Bathroom Water Damage: What Tinley Park Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. In Tinley Park, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Signs to Look For

  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
