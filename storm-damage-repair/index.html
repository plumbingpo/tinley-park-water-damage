Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge
  • Branches break in high wind, so keep nearby trees pruned away from the roof and siding
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Homes in hurricane country benefit from shutters or impact-rated window glass

Why Hire a Professional for Storm Damage Repair?

The repairs a storm leaves behind are tangled together, and sorting them out correctly calls for an experienced restoration crew. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the route water took and find all the areas it reached.

Emergency board-up and tarping also take proper technique to be effective. Tarps put on the wrong way may funnel water into the building or tear loose in the wind. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

Signs You Need Storm Damage Repair in Tinley Park

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Damp, earthy odors that appear a few days after the storm
  • Damaged or displaced flashing around roof penetrations, valleys or edges
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • Water getting past doors, windows and wall penetrations when wind pushes the rain
  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Standing water in attic spaces, especially by skylights and chimneys
