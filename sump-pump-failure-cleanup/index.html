Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Tinley Park: What to Know

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Boxes and Stored Items

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Furnace and Water Heater

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Bottom Plates and Studs

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Hours or days of standing water ahead of extraction and drying.
  • Whether it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, since that decides what gets thrown out and disinfected.
  • Ongoing seepage that requires us to run a temporary pump in the pit.
  • Water depth, along with the square footage that flooded.
