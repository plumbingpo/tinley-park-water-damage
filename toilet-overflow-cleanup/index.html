Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Tinley Park: What to Know

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. What matters most is what was in it. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

In Tinley Park, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. If it is dripping or sagging, switch off power to that room and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Block the SpreadOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Water spreads to the vanity toe kick and the lower edge of the drywall, where it wicks upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Ceiling Below

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Soaked Subfloor

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Our Process

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

1

Identify the Category

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

We extract standing water, pull contaminated carpet pad, baseboards or drywall where needed, and lift the toilet if water has gone under the base and flange.

3

Clean and Disinfect

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. For Category 3 jobs, disinfection comes first and drying equipment starts only afterward.

4

Dry and Verify

We set drying equipment in the bathroom and in the room underneath if water reached it. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
