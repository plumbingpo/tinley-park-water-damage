Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Tinley Park Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Our Process

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.
